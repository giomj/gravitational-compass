## Purpose

Establish the coordinate frame, the clock, and the honest accounting of time under which all subsequent HRC milestones operate.

## The Frame

**Heliocentric.** The Sun is the coordinate anchor. This is not a philosophical claim about the Sun's importance; it is a mechanical choice about the reference frame in which our orbital and gravitational calculations are simplest.

Note: The Sun is the coordinate anchor. **It is not exempt from the Preamble.** Reverence extends to every creature. The physical/spiritual distinction stays intact — the Sun does not become sacred by being the anchor; it becomes the anchor because it is convenient and physically legible.

## The Clock

**SI second.** The International System second, defined by the caesium-133 hyperfine transition, is the standard time unit.

## Relativistic Corrections (Einstein co-signs this section)

Time in HRC is not a scalar. It is a field that varies with:

- **Gravitational potential** — clocks near massive bodies run slower (gravitational time dilation).
- **Relative velocity** — clocks in motion relative to the observer run slower (special-relativistic time dilation).

For most Earth-bound biosensor engineering, Newtonian time is a sufficient approximation. For satellite work, off-world work, and any precision experiment on the timescale of years, relativistic corrections apply.

Newton's approximation is safe when:
- \\( v \\ll c \\) (velocity much less than the speed of light)
- \\( \\Phi \\ll c^2 \\) (gravitational potential much less than \\( c^2 \\))

Newton's approximation is *unsafe* when:
- Satellite orbital clocks (GPS corrections are relativistic)
- Any measurement across the Earth's surface at sub-nanosecond precision
- Any comparison to a solar-frame clock over decades

## Complementarity (Bohr co-signs this section)

**Clock time and observer time are a complementary pair.** The SI second gives us a universal external clock. But the human observer experiences time subjectively, and that subjective experience is not reducible to the clock.

HRC treats both descriptions as necessary and non-reducible. The clock tells us when. The observer tells us when it mattered.

## Refutation Surface

**This milestone would yield under the following conditions:**

1. A demonstrated experimental result showing SI-second definition is unreliable at HRC-relevant precision.
2. A discovery that the heliocentric frame is unstable in a way that breaks orbital-mechanics predictions we rely on (extremely unlikely, but named for honesty).
3. A determination by the observer that a different reference frame better serves the sacred creature.
